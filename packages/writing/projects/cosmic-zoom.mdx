## Project overview

Cosmic Zoom was an online science exhibition organized by the International Centre for Theoretical Sciences (ICTS-TIFR), Bengaluru. It invited visitors to travel through the spatial scales of the Universe, from subatomic particles to the observable cosmos, and to notice connections between worlds that are usually studied separately.

I joined ICTS as a junior engineer intern, working with exhibition designers, scientists, event planners, illustrators, and motion designers. My contribution was web design and development: helping turn the curatorial idea into an online experience people could explore. The original `cosmic-zoom.in` domain is no longer the reliable way to access the exhibition; a [backup of the experience is available here](https://bash.ram.codes), with team credits on its [About page](https://bash.ram.codes/about).

## Problem and exhibition context

The exhibition needed to make enormous differences in scale understandable and inviting to a broad audience. Visitors were meant to move from the very small to the very large, encountering scientific ideas across physics, astronomy, biology, ecology, and other fields along the way.

The work was also part of a public outreach effort during the COVID-19 pandemic, when in-person exhibitions and events were difficult to run. ICTS described Cosmic Zoom as a virtual exhibition and a curtain-raiser for the proposed Bengaluru Science Habba. The online format made it possible to combine an explorable exhibition with a program of talks, workshops, and conversations.

## Planning and design

The central design challenge was to make scale itself a navigation idea. The archived experience includes pages for subjects such as subatomic particles, atoms, cells, insects, Earth, the Solar System, stars, galaxies, and the cosmic microwave background. Navigation links let visitors move between smaller and larger scales, turning the exhibition’s scientific theme into a way to explore the site.

This required coordination between the structure of the website and the story being told by the content. Scientists and science communicators shaped the information; illustrators and motion designers developed the visual material; and the web experience had to bring those pieces together in an understandable sequence. I worked on the implementation side with the exhibition design team, rather than authoring or independently curating the scientific content.

## Implementation and key features

The website organized content as an exploratory journey through progressively different scales. Visitors could enter at a subject and continue to smaller or larger topics, including from the Solar System toward stars and galaxies, or from cells toward atoms and subatomic particles.

The broader exhibition connected the scale journey to a wide range of research and public engagement topics. ICTS describes content and events spanning astronomy, particle physics, quantum phenomena, cell biology, forests, extraterrestrial life, climate, insects, marine life, and ecology. The archived site also includes an events destination alongside the exhibition pages.

My role was junior engineer on web design and development. I helped build the website that carried this exhibition experience and worked alongside the specialists responsible for curation, science, exhibition design, graphics, and animation.

## Lessons learned and takeaways

- Let the subject shape the interface. Here, the scale of the Universe became the navigation model, helping the site express its central idea through interaction.
- Treat collaboration as part of implementation. The quality of a science exhibition depends on how well the web experience supports curatorial, scientific, editorial, and visual work.
- Design for the audience’s first question: where am I, and what can I explore next? Clear relationships between neighboring topics help visitors make sense of a large content space.
- Plan around media and content production early. Illustrations, animation, scientific review, and event information all affect how pages are structured and delivered.
- Keep attribution visible. A multidisciplinary exhibition is a collective result, and clear credits give proper context to an individual engineering contribution.
